What this is

A self-contained practice app for Turkish at beginner level (A1–A2): a grammar reference you can consult, grammar drills, and vocabulary flashcards that remember what you know and serve you what you don't.

It is one HTML file. It works offline, on a computer or a phone, needs no account, no cookies and no tracking, and sends nothing anywhere. Your progress is stored only in this browser.

Quick start

1. Open Vocabulary and press Smart.

2. Try to recall the answer, then click the card (or press space) to reveal it.

3. Grade yourself honestly: I know it strongly · I guessed · I don't know. The scheduling relies on honest answers; a guess counts as not knowing yet.

4. Keep going until the round says Round complete!. A round is at most 50 cards, usually fewer. Press Smart again for a fresh round whenever you come back.

Do the same on the Grammar drills tab for conjugations and sentence patterns, and use the Grammar reference whenever a drill or card confuses you. On the drills tab you can also switch the answer mode to build tiles and assemble each answer from its pieces.

Content is organized in eight levels that unlock as you practice, so you are never flooded with material above your level. Everything can also be unlocked (or re-locked) by hand on the Help tab.

Why it works this way

The design follows the vocabulary-learning research: actively recalling before revealing beats re-reading; grading yourself creates the data for scheduling; practicing a medium-sized block (not five words over and over) spaces out each word's repetitions, which is what builds long-term memory; and new words arrive a few at a time, in roughly the order textbooks teach them, so you always work at the edge of what you know, not on what is trivially easy or hopelessly hard. Details and sources are on the About tab.

Sections beyond your level are dimmed. They stay fully readable, and they unlock as you practice; you can also lock or unlock any level yourself on the Help tab.

Vowel harmony: the rule behind every suffix

2-way (e-type suffixes: -ler/-lar, -de/-da, …): last vowel a ı o u → suffix has a; last vowel e i ö ü → suffix has e.

4-way (i-type suffixes: -dı/-di/-du/-dü, personal endings): a ı → ı · e i → i · o u → u · ö ü → ü

ev → evler, evde · yol → yollar, yolda · gördüm, baktım, geldim, buldum

Consonant softening

A vowel-suffix after p ç t k often softens it: kitap → kitabı, ağaç → ağacı, gerçek → gerçeği, çok → çoğu.

Not a law: most one-syllable words and many t-final words stay hard: saat → saati, at → atı, sepet → sepeti. Learn softening word by word; the dictionary form plus one suffixed form tells you.

Possession: my, your, his/her…

çantam (my) · çantan (your) · çantası (his/her) · çantamız (our) · çantanız (your pl.) · çantaları (their)

The pronoun is usually dropped: Çantam burada. My bag is here.

"I have / I don't have" uses possession + var/yok: Arabam var. Zamanım yok. I have a car. I have no time.

Var / Yok: there is, there isn't

Evde ekmek var. There is bread at home.

Evde ekmek yok. There is no bread at home.

Evde ekmek var mı? Is there bread at home?

Gerek yok. / Lazım değil. No need. / Not needed.

Plurals: -ler / -lar

2-way harmony: front vowels take -ler, back vowels take -lar: ev → evler, araba → arabalar, gün → günler, kutu → kutular.

No plural after a number or amount word: the number already says how many: üç kutu three boxes (not üç kutular), çok araba many cars, kaç kişi? how many people?

Other suffixes stack after the plural: komşu-lar-ımız-la with our neighbors.

Every finite verb is built the same way: stem + tense suffix + person ending. Drop -mak/-mek to get the stem: gelmek → gel-, yaşamak → yaşa-. The tense suffix obeys vowel harmony with the last vowel of the stem; the person ending harmonises with the tense suffix. Details for each tense below.

Present continuous: -(i)yor

Use: what is happening right now, and (like English) near-future plans: Şimdi çalışıyorum. I am working now.

Build it: stem + ı/i/u/ü + yor + person. The linking vowel follows 4-way harmony with the stem's last vowel. If the stem already ends in a or e, that vowel narrows first: yaşa- → yaşıyor, bekle- → bekliyor.

Present simple (aorist): -r / -ir / -er

Use: habits, general truths, offers and promises, not what is happening now: Her sabah çay içerim. I drink tea every morning.

Build it: vowel-ending stems just add -r (yaşa- → yaşar); most one-syllable stems take -ar/-er (gel- → gelir is one of a few exceptions you learn by ear); longer stems take -ır/-ir/-ur/-ür (çalış- → çalışır).

The same endings work on nouns and adjectives: Hastaydı. He was ill. Evdeydik. We were at home.

Past (reported): -miş

Use: things you didn't witness yourself: hearsay, inference, surprise: Ali evlenmiş! (I hear / apparently) Ali got married!

Build it: stem + mış/miş/muş/müş + person (4-way harmony). Same person endings as -di: gelmişim, gelmişsin, gelmiş, gelmişiz, gelmişsiniz, gelmişler.

Negative: gelmemiş. Question: Gelmiş mi?

Passive: -il / -in

Use: when the doer is unknown or unimportant: Kapı kapatıldı. The door was closed.

Build it: add to the stem, before the tense suffix:

Stem ends in…SuffixExample

Participle -an / -en: "the one who..."

Use: to describe a noun with a whole clause — English "who/that does X". The clause comes before the noun, like one big adjective:

Çok iyi kahve yapan bir kafe. A café that makes very good coffee.

Grammar drills

Numbers

Practice days

Everything here is self-referenced and criterion-based: a word counts as mastered only after verified strong answers on three different days, in both directions. Minutes are deliberately not tracked - regular days, not long sessions, are what move these bars.

Levels

Content unlocks level by level as you practice: the next level opens when about 60% of the current level's words are ones you answer correctly. This is a default pace, not a wall. Lock or unlock any level above, in any order; reference sections are always readable.

Controls

Smart builds a practice round from your history: your recent misses first, then the words you half-know, mixed 2:1 with up to ten new words, plus a few mastered ones as review. Answering I know it strongly retires a card from the round; the round ends when nothing is left.

Shuffle is a plain random run through the whole selected set. In every deck, answering I know it strongly sets the card aside like a known card from a physical pile - the pile shrinks, misses stay in it, and emptying it completes the set.

Weak first reorders the set so your most-missed cards come first, unseen cards in the middle, well-known ones last.

Answer modes: reveal is the classic flashcard flow. On the drills tab, build tiles makes you assemble the answer from morpheme or word tiles (one plausible wrong tile mixed in); a correct build counts as a strong answer. Choices (both tabs) is multiple choice: right picks count as recognition, which helps but never completes mastery. Fill the blank (vocabulary) hides the word's form inside its own example sentence with the English as the cue - a production exercise, so a correct fill counts as a strong answer on the English → Türkçe side. The drills tab also has a Number relations topic: generated questions like "beşten sonra ____ gelir" with fresh numbers every time - you compute the answer instead of recalling it. Timed round (vocabulary) is a speed drill over words you already know - a six-second countdown per card, wrong taps count as misses but slow answers never do. It trains fast access, the skill conversation actually needs. Cards a mode cannot serve simply show as reveal cards.

On the Vocabulary tab the two selectors choose the practice direction (Turkish→English, English→Turkish, or mixed; mixed shows each card in the direction you are weaker at, because recognizing a word and producing it are learned separately) and the word set (everything, core words, fixed expressions, or the most frequent book words). The defaults are the broadest: mixed direction, all words.

Grading and card states

After revealing, grade your recall. Each card keeps lifetime counts, shown once revealed ("strong 2 · guessed 1 · missed 3"), and a state:

StateMeaning
new cardnever graded
learninglast answer was a miss, or the record is net negative
almost thereseen, but not yet 3 strong answers in a row
masteredstrong answers on 3 different days in a row; one miss resets the streak

I guessed counts as not-yet-known: guessed cards stay in heavy rotation until you can answer them cold.

Answering the same card strongly many times in one sitting does not speed up mastery. Only the first strong answer of the day advances the streak. Memory is built by coming back tomorrow, not by repeating today. Vocabulary cards track each direction separately: knowing bardak→"glass" doesn't mean you can produce "glass"→bardak.

About this app

Built as a single offline HTML file for beginner Turkish study. The vocabulary ( cards) and grammar topics follow a standard beginner textbook path; every card carries an idiomatic example sentence. New words are introduced in the order the textbooks teach them, weighted by how often each word actually occurs in beginner texts, with everyday basics (gelmek, gitmek, yapmak…) pinned early.

The research behind Smart mode

Retrieval practice: actively recalling before revealing strengthens memory far more than re-reading (the "testing effect"); the benefit grows with repeated retrieval rounds.

Block size: practicing in one larger block beats splitting into tiny piles: small sets feel easier but give no retention advantage, because what matters is the spacing between repetitions of the same word (Nakata & Webb 2016, SSLA; Kornell 2009, Applied Cognitive Psychology). Hence rounds of up to 50 cards with a 20-card "edge" block.

Introduction rate: about 10 new items per session, with older material mixed back in (cumulative practice improves retention; Maie et al. 2025, TESOL Quarterly). Hence 10 new + 5 review per round.

Working the edge: time goes to material just beyond current mastery (misses and half-known words) rather than what is already easy or still far too hard (zone of proximal development; desirable difficulties).

Known limitations / roadmap

Scheduling is day-granular: mastered cards resurface by least-recently-seen review rather than by a full spaced-repetition interval model.
